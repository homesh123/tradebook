Trading risk management rules that keep you in the game

Updated October 31, 2026

Short answer: Good risk management means deciding before every trade how much you can lose, sizing the position so a stop-out costs exactly that amount, and stopping for the day when a pre-set loss limit is reached. Most professional traders risk 0.5%–2% of their account per trade.

How much should I risk per trade?

Most professional traders risk between 0.5% and 2% of their account on a single trade; 1% is the most common rule. At 1% risk, ten losses in a row cost about 10% of the account – painful but survivable. At 10% risk, the same streak would cost about 65%.

How do I calculate position size?

Position size = (account balance × risk %) ÷ (stop distance × point value).

  • Stocks: $20,000 account, 1% risk = $200. Entry $50, stop $48 (distance $2). Size = 200 ÷ 2 = 100 shares.
  • Futures (ES, $50 per point): $50,000 account, 1% = $500. Stop 5 points away = $250 per contract. Size = 2 contracts.
  • Forex (EUR/USD, $10 per pip per standard lot): $10,000 account, 1% = $100. Stop 20 pips = $200 per lot. Size = 0.5 lots.

What is a daily loss limit and why use one?

A daily loss limit is the maximum you allow yourself to lose in one day; when you reach it, you stop trading until tomorrow. It protects you from tilt – the spiral of emotional trades after losses – which is where most account-destroying days come from. A common starting point is 2–3× your per-trade risk.

Why limit the number of trades per day?

A maximum number of trades per day stops overtrading: forcing low-quality setups out of boredom or to recover losses. Pick a number that matches how many A+ setups your strategy realistically produces in a session, typically 2–5 for day traders.

Frequently asked questions

What is the 1% rule in trading?

The 1% rule means never risking more than 1% of your account on a single trade. With a $10,000 account the maximum loss per trade is $100, and position size is chosen so that hitting the stop loss costs exactly that.

What is a good daily loss limit?

A common daily loss limit is 2–3 times your per-trade risk, for example 2–3% of the account if you risk 1% per trade. The exact number matters less than stopping every single time you reach it.

Should every trade have a stop loss?

Yes. Without a stop loss you cannot size the position, measure R-multiples or cap the loss. Decide the stop before entry and place it as a real order.
